01 — The basics

What is an NFC tag?

A Near Field Communication tag is a coin-sized sticker, card, or keychain fob with a tiny chip and a coiled antenna inside — no battery, no power button.

How it actually works

  • The tag is passive — it has no battery. Your phone's NFC radio sends out a small burst of energy that briefly powers the chip.
  • While powered, the chip transmits the tiny bit of data stored on it — usually a web link, a phone number, or a short instruction.
  • Range is intentionally short: about 2–4 cm, so you have to deliberately tap, not walk past one.
  • Tags follow the ISO/IEC 14443 standard, the same family used by contactless payment cards and transit passes.
ChipMemoryBest for
NTAG213180 bytesA single link, phone number, or short action
NTAG215540 bytesMultiple chained actions, Wi-Fi credentials
NTAG216924 bytesLarge multi-step automations, vCard contacts

Most sticker packs sold for home automation use NTAG213 or NTAG215 — plenty for the examples below.

03 — The everyday flow

How do you use NFC tags?

Once a tag is programmed, using it has nothing to do with an app — it's just a tap.

📲

Turn on NFC

Check that NFC is enabled in your phone's quick settings — it's on by default on most modern phones.

📍

Place the tag

Stick it somewhere the action makes sense: your nightstand, front door, car dashboard, or desk.

✋

Tap your phone

Hold the back of your phone (where the NFC antenna sits) against the tag for about a second.

⚡

Action fires

Your phone reads the stored instruction and runs it immediately — no app needs to be open first.

04 — Step by step

How to program an NFC tag

This exact sequence works for any single action inside NFC Tools.

Open NFC Tools and go to "Write"

Launch the app and tap the Write tab along the bottom — this is where new data gets prepared before it's saved to a tag.

Tap "Add a record"

A record is one instruction. You'll pick a category — Basic (text, URL, phone number), Application, Social Networks, Home Automation, or Custom.

Choose your task type and fill in the details

For example, pick "Phone number" and type in the number, or "Wi-Fi" and enter a network name and password.

Confirm the record

Tap "OK" — the record now appears in your write queue, ready to be saved. You can add more records here for a multi-task tag.

Tap "Write" and hold your phone to the tag

Press the blue Write button, then hold the phone against the blank tag for one to two seconds until you feel a vibration or see a success message.

Test it

Pull the phone away, then tap it back on the tag. If the action runs, you're done. If not, re-check the record and try again — cheap tags occasionally need a firmer, more centered tap.

🔒
Optional: once a tag works the way you want, NFC Tools lets you lock it under Other Tools → Write, permanently preventing future edits — useful for tags you're giving to someone else.

06 — Example

Multi-task example: a "Good Night" tag

NFC Tools can hold several records on one tag. On tap, each one fires in order — turning this into a tiny automation remote. This "Good Night" tag on a nightstand turns off the living room light, turns on a dim bedroom lamp, and switches on Do Not Disturb.

1️⃣
Record 1 — URLsmart-home-app://scene/living-room-off
2️⃣
Record 2 — URLsmart-home-app://scene/bedroom-lamp-dim
3️⃣
Record 3 — Application launchToggle Do Not Disturb (via Home Automation → Other)

Each record was added with "Add another record" before writing, so all three save to the same tag and run back-to-back.

💡
Smart bulbs and plugs almost always support triggering via a deep link, IFTTT webhook, or Shortcuts/Tasker action — put that link into an NFC Tools "URL" or "Launch app" record for each device.
