Moving Average Crossover (Golden Cross Strategy)

The Moving Average Crossover, often referred to as the Golden Cross Strategy, is a popular technical analysis tool used in stock trading. It identifies potential bullish trends by detecting when a shorter-term moving average (MA) crosses above a longer-term MA. This crossover signals increasing upward momentum, suggesting a possible shift from a bearish to a bullish market phase. In this report, we focus on shorter-term MAs (5-day, 20-day, and 50-day) for swing trading opportunities, which can help spot medium-term trends lasting days to months.

Column Explanations

The report includes the following columns, each providing key data on moving averages and price positioning:

  • ma-5 day: The 5-day Simple Moving Average (SMA), calculated as the average closing price over the last 5 trading days. This short-term MA is highly sensitive to recent price changes and is used to detect quick momentum shifts.
  • ma-20 day: The 20-day Simple Moving Average (SMA), representing the average closing price over the last 20 trading days (about one month). It serves as an intermediate trend indicator and is often watched for early crossover signals with longer MAs.
  • ma-50 day: The 50-day Simple Moving Average (SMA), based on the average closing price over the last 50 trading days (roughly 2-3 months). This is a key medium-term trend line; prices or shorter MAs crossing above it indicate strengthening bullish momentum.
  • pct above ma-50 day: The percentage by which the current stock price is above (or below) the 50-day MA. Calculated as [(Current Price - 50-day MA) / 50-day MA] × 100. Positive values suggest an uptrend, while negative values indicate bearish pressure.
