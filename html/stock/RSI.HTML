RSI (Relative Strength Index) - Oversold/Overbought

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements on a scale from 0 to 100. This report focuses on identifying oversold stocks (RSI < 30), which may indicate that the stock has been sold off excessively and could be poised for a rebound or "bounce back." It's a popular tool for traders looking for potential reversal points in downtrends, especially in volatile markets. Note that RSI can also signal overbought conditions (RSI > 70), but this screener emphasizes oversold opportunities.

Column Explanations

The report includes the following columns to help you evaluate oversold stocks:

  • Stock code: The ticker symbol or code for the stock (e.g., AAPL for Apple Inc.). This is the unique identifier used on exchanges.
  • Last price: The most recent closing price of the stock. This gives you the current market value and helps assess affordability or potential upside.
  • RSI_14 value: The RSI value calculated over a 14-period timeframe (typically 14 days, the standard setting). Lower values (below 30) suggest oversold conditions, while values above 70 indicate overbought.

Tips for Picking Stocks for Returns in 1-3 Months

For aiming at returns (e.g., 8-15% or more) within a 1-3 month window, focus on deeply oversold stocks that show signs of reversal. Use the RSI_14 value as your primary filter, but combine with price action, volume, and market trends for better results. Here's a guide:

Key Focus What to Look For in RSI_14 Why It Matters for 1-3 Months
RSI_14 Value 10-25 (deeply oversold); ideally <20 for stronger bounces Extremely low RSI often signals capitulation, leading to quick reversals. In 1-3 months, these can yield sharp recoveries as sentiment shifts.
Avoid RSI_14 >30 or persistently <10 (extreme weakness) Above 30 isn't oversold; below 10 might indicate a prolonged downtrend rather than a short-term bounce.
Combine with Last Price Stocks with stable or rising price despite low RSI Look for bullish divergence (price bottoms while RSI rises) for confirmation of a 1-3 month upswing.
Additional Tips High volume on low RSI days; sector strength Increases conviction for medium-term holds. Use stop-losses below recent lows to manage risk.

This strategy works best in bull or recovering markets—oversold RSI in bear markets can stay low longer. Always diversify and research fundamentals.
